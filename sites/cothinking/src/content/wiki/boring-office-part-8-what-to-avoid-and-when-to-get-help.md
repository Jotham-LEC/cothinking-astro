## Three mistakes to avoid

### Hosting your own email

A mail server of your own, or the free mailboxes that come with cheap web hosting, saves a little money and costs you delivery. Gmail and Outlook trust established senders, so a small server's mail lands in spam far more often, and keeping it secure never ends.

- **Instead:** use Google Workspace or Microsoft 365 ([[Setting up IT for a small company, Part 4: Email and files|Part 4]]).
- **If you already do:** move the mailboxes to one of the two suites, then set up SPF, DKIM, and DMARC. See [[Why are our emails going to spam?]].

### Keeping customers on staff phones

When customers' details live only on a salesperson's personal WhatsApp account or phone number, the company doesn't own its leads. When the salesperson leaves, the customers go with them.

- **Instead:** record every customer in a CRM that the company owns, and give sales staff company numbers or WhatsApp Business accounts where possible. See [[How to connect WhatsApp enquiries to your CRM]].
- **If you already do:** export the contacts into the CRM now, while the staff are still with you, and make the CRM the only place where customers are recorded from then on.

### Letting someone else register the domain

Whoever holds the domain controls the website and the email ([[Setting up IT for a small company, Part 2: The domain and accounts|Part 2]]). A domain registered by a web designer, an agency, or a relative, in their own name, puts the company's website and email in someone else's hands.

- **Instead:** register the domain yourself, in the company's name.
- **If you already have:** ask the holder to transfer it to an account in the company's name while relations are good. See [[Website ownership checklist: who should hold your domain, hosting and analytics]].

## When to get help

Get help when any of these is true:

- IT maintenance takes more than two days a month.
- It regularly takes up your weekends.
- A single problem takes several days to resolve.

Your time is then worth more on the business itself.

## Freelancer or managed IT

- **A freelancer** suits occasional projects and problems, such as a migration or a new laptop setup, and costs nothing between jobs.
- **A managed IT provider** suits a company that needs regular maintenance, a helpdesk, and someone accountable for response times.

Either way, the company keeps every account in its own name, and the provider works through users added to those accounts. See [[How to choose an IT support provider for a small company]] and [[Questions to ask an IT support provider before you sign]].

## Before you hire

A provider starts sooner, and charges less, when you can hand over:

1. The access register ([[Setting up IT for a small company, Part 2: The domain and accounts|Part 2]]).
2. A list of laptops, printers, and other equipment, with their ages.
3. A short description of the problem or the work, and how urgent it is.

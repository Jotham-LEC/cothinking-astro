A website maintenance plan should state six things in writing: hosting, backups, updates, monitoring, changes and reply times, and renewal and exit terms. A plan that says only "maintenance" is a fee for an unknown amount of attention.

## Why a website needs maintaining

The software under a website stops receiving security fixes on a published schedule, and a site left alone becomes easier to break into each year.

- **PHP**, which WordPress runs on, gets two years of active support and two of security fixes per version. PHP 8.2's security fixes end on 31 December 2026 ([php.net](https://www.php.net/supported-versions.php)).
- **WordPress** updates its own security releases automatically, but plugins and themes only "in special cases" ([WordPress](https://developer.wordpress.org/advanced-administration/upgrade/upgrading/)). Plugins carry most of the risk.

## The six things a plan should cover

| Item | What the plan should say |
| --- | --- |
| **1. Hosting** | Where, on whose account, what is included (SSL, email, staging), and what happens if you cancel |
| **2. Backups** | Files and database; daily or weekly; kept off the server; for how long; the last restore test |
| **3. Updates** | Who updates core, plugins, themes, and PHP; how often; tested afterwards; major upgrades included or quoted |
| **4. Monitoring** | Downtime, certificates, changed files, malware; who acts; malware removal included or not |
| **5. Changes and reply times** | Hours included; carry-over; rate for extra hours; what counts as new work; reply and fix times |
| **6. Renewal and exit** | Next year's price, auto-renewal, notice period, and the backup, files, and logins you get on leaving |

What to press on:

- **Restore tests.** Malaysia's National Cyber Security Agency advises backups "on a separate media and stored offline at an alternate site" ([NACSA](https://nacsa.gov.my/advisory9.php)). Ask for a test restore once or twice a year, and its date.
- **Updates.** "Weekly, checked on staging first" is an answer; "we keep everything up to date" isn't.
- **Logins.** Two-step verification for every admin, no shared accounts, and former staff removed.
- **Reply times.** Ask for the reply and fix times in writing, and whether they are promised or only a target. For a small business's site, a reply within one or two working days and a same-day start on an outage are common.
- **Ownership.** Hosting in your company's name (see the [[Website ownership checklist: who should hold your domain, hosting and analytics]]). A plan with no exit terms is one of the [[Red flags in a website or IT quotation]].

## What plans cost in Malaysia

Published prices, October 2026:

| Provider | Price | Includes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Agency A | RM699 a year | Domain and hosting renewal, "website version updates" |
| Agency A | RM1,500, prepaid | 12 hours for edits, uploads, backups, plugin updates, a security check |
| Agency B | RM550 a year + SST | 1 to 5 pages, 3 hours of maintenance; 1-year minimum |
| Agency B | RM1,500 a year + SST | 6 to 10 pages, 10 hours of maintenance; 1-year minimum |
| Agency C | RM550 a month | Weekly updates and off-site backups, hardening, uptime and SSL monitoring |
| Agency C | RM1,200 a month | Adds daily backups, daily malware scans with removal, staging, monthly reports |

*Checked in October 2026. Names withheld; sources held on file.*

The first four are mostly renewal or hours for edits; the last two are weekly security and backup work. A site that takes enquiries, runs a shop, or would embarrass you if defaced probably needs the second kind.

To compare plans, fill in the six rows for each, plus the two-year cost with tax and licences. A blank after you have asked in writing is an answer.

> [!IMPORTANT] Editing in-house doesn't replace a plan
> Staff can edit a page, but not restore a hacked database.

Hire a package shop for a small, standard site; a freelancer for a well-defined job; an agency for a bigger or connected site; and someone in-house when the site changes weekly and is central to how you sell. Most Malaysian businesses of up to 50 staff do best with an outside provider who builds and looks after the site, and a named person inside who owns it.

## The four options compared

| | Package shop | Freelancer | Agency | In-house hire |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Best for | A small, standard site, quickly | A well-defined job | A bigger or connected site; design, copy, and code together | A site that changes weekly |
| Cost | About RM2,000 to RM3,300 for 5 or 6 pages, then yearly renewals | Per project or per hour | From about RM5,000 into the tens of thousands, plus a yearly maintenance fee | A salary every month (median RM4,000 for a web developer) |
| Main risk | Template limits; accounts in the shop's name; a builder you can't take elsewhere; renewals that rise | One person: illness, other clients, moving on | Senior rates for junior work | One person, with narrow skills |
| Continuity | Depends on the shop | Weakest | Strongest | Good while they stay |

## When a package shop fits

You need a credible site soon, a handful of pages that rarely change, connected to nothing. For a new company, a small trading business, or a firm that gets customers by referral, an agency at five times the price is waste.

## When a freelancer fits

Often the best value: one experienced person, no account manager, a lower rate than an agency. Choose one when:

- the scope is clear (a rebuild of a known site, a product catalogue with quotation requests, a set of landing pages, or a WordPress theme fixed);
- you can judge their past work;
- the business won't stop if they are away for two weeks.

The one-person risk falls if every account is in your company's name and the freelancer writes down what runs where, so another developer could take over. [[Website ownership checklist: who should hold your domain, hosting and analytics]] lists the accounts to check.

## When an agency fits

- The site is more than a brochure, with a CRM connection or several languages.
- You need design and copy as well as code.
- You want a contract with a company rather than a person, with someone to call if your usual contact leaves.

Ask who will do your project, and what the yearly fee covers; [[What a website maintenance plan should include]] has the list.

## When to hire in-house

Hire when the site changes constantly: weekly content, campaign landing pages, and tests on forms and offers. At that pace an outside provider's turnaround is the bottleneck.

What it costs, from JobStreet's salary data (ranges employers put in job ads, as published in October 2026):

| Role | Median a month | Middle half of ads |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Web developer](https://my.jobstreet.com/career-advice/role/web-developer/salary) | RM4,000 | RM2,750 to RM5,250 |
| [Digital marketing executive](https://my.jobstreet.com/career-advice/role/digital-marketing-executive/salary) | RM3,750 | |

Add EPF and SOCSO, a laptop, software, and hiring time. Twelve months at RM4,000 is RM48,000 before any of that, more than most small companies spend on a website in several years. And one person rarely has every skill: a developer may not write, a marketer may not code, and neither may know email deliverability or server security.

### What in-house gives you

As Head of Global Marketing at Moojing, a China-based market-intelligence company, I ran its search work from the inside (see [[Appearing in Google and AI search]]). An outside provider could have made the same fixes, but wouldn't have known which pages sales sent to prospects or which questions buyers kept asking. If your site doesn't need that judgement every week, a salary pays for the wrong thing.

## What works for most businesses of up to 50 staff

An outside provider builds the site and looks after hosting, updates, backups and security on a maintenance plan. A named person inside the company owns it, usually in marketing or sales operations: they hold the admin accounts with a colleague, edit pages, write the content and act as the provider's one contact. Bigger changes are quoted as projects, so the yearly cost stays predictable.

If you then change provider, you change one relationship, not your whole website.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Whoever builds it, someone inside the company must own the website: hold the logins, know the renewal dates, and approve the changes.

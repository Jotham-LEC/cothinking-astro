The Personal Data Protection (Amendment) Act 2024 came into force in three stages in 2025. If your sales and marketing teams handle customers' personal data, four changes matter to you as the owner: fines of up to RM1 million for breaching the data protection principles, breach reports to the Commissioner within 72 hours, a data protection officer (DPO) for companies that hold a lot of personal data or track people for advertising, and a customer's right to move their data elsewhere.

> [!NOTE]
> This isn't legal advice. It is my reading of the Act and the Commissioner's guidelines, written for business owners. If a decision depends on it, ask a lawyer who practises data protection law.

## What changed, and when

The amendment is [Act A1727](https://lom.agc.gov.my/ilims/upload/portal/akta/outputaktap/2430673_BI/Act%20A1727.pdf). Its commencement dates are in the [Federal Gazette, P.U. (B) 522](https://christopherleeong.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/PDPA-Amendment-PUB.pdf) of 24 December 2024.

| In force | What changed | Where in the Act |
|---|---|---|
| 1 January 2025 | Notices under the Act can be served electronically; housekeeping | ss. 7, 11, 13, 14 |
| 1 April 2025 | "Data user" becomes "data controller" | s. 2 |
| 1 April 2025 | Biometric data becomes sensitive personal data | s. 3 |
| 1 April 2025 | Data processors must follow the Security Principle; the fine for breaching the principles rises from RM300,000 and two years to RM1 million and three years | s. 4 |
| 1 April 2025 | The "whitelist" for sending data abroad is removed | s. 12 |
| 1 June 2025 | Data protection officers | s. 6 (new s. 12A) |
| 1 June 2025 | Data breach notification | s. 6 (new s. 12B) |
| 1 June 2025 | Right to data portability | s. 9 (new s. 43A) |

## The 2010 basics still apply

These were already in the [Personal Data Protection Act 2010](https://www.pdp.gov.my/ppdpv1/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/UNDANG-UNDANG-MALAYSIA_AKTA_PERLINDUNGAN_DATA_PERIBADI_2010_709_MALAY_AND-ENG_V2022.pdf), and marketing teams still get them wrong:

- **A notice in Malay and English** (s. 7(3)). An English-only form is a gap.
- **Say why you collect the data**, who you share it with, and how to reach you, when you first collect it (s. 7).
- **Stop direct marketing when asked in writing** (s. 43). An email platform's unsubscribe link covers email. A salesperson's WhatsApp list doesn't.

## Report a breach within 72 hours

Section 12B requires you to notify the Commissioner "as soon as practicable", and to tell affected people without unnecessary delay if the breach is likely to cause them significant harm. The Commissioner's [Data Breach Notification Guideline](https://www.pdp.gov.my/ppdpv1/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/GP_DBN_ENG.pdf) (25 February 2025) sets the numbers:

<img class="wiki-logo-dark" src="/media/diagrams/pdpa-for-marketing-and-sales-breach-dark.svg" alt="" loading="lazy" decoding="async">

- **72 hours** from the breach to notify the Commissioner, through the [online form](https://www.pdp.gov.my/ppdpv1/en/report-dbn/). If you are late, explain why in writing.
- **Seven days** after that to tell the affected people.
- **Significant harm** includes a risk of financial loss or identity fraud, sensitive personal data, and any breach affecting **more than 1,000 people**. Most marketing databases cross that line.
- **A register** of every breach, including those you didn't report, kept for at least two years.
- **Up to RM250,000**, two years' imprisonment, or both, for failing to notify the Commissioner.

Marketing breaches rarely look like hacking:

- a lead list exported to a personal Gmail account;
- a salesperson's CRM login phished;
- a sign-up spreadsheet shared as "anyone with the link";
- a former agency that still has the admin password to your email platform.

## Do you need a data protection officer?

Section 12A requires a DPO, and the Commissioner's [DPO guideline](https://www.pdp.gov.my/ppdpv1/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/GP_DPO_ENG.pdf) limits it to processing that involves any of these:

- personal data of **more than 20,000** people;
- sensitive personal data, including financial information, of **more than 10,000** people;
- **regular and systematic monitoring** of personal data.

> [!IMPORTANT] Tracking for advertising can count as monitoring
> The guideline's first example of regular and systematic monitoring is tracking and profiling people "online or offline for purposes of behavioural advertising". If your business runs that kind of tracking itself, take advice.

For a business of up to 50 staff, the 20,000 test is the usual trigger. Count the unique people in your CRM, email platform, and old spreadsheets together.

The DPO:

- can be part-time, or outsourced under a service contract;
- should be resident in Malaysia (at least 180 days a year) or easily contactable;
- should be proficient in Malay and English;
- must be registered with the Commissioner within 21 days of appointment, on the [DPO registration page](https://www.pdp.gov.my/ppdpv1/en/data-protection-officer-registration-guide/).

## Data portability

Section 43A lets a customer ask you, by written notice sent electronically, to send their data to another data controller. It is "subject to technical feasibility and compatibility of the data format", and the deadline is left to be prescribed: check pdp.gov.my for whether one has been set. Either way, you need to know every system that holds a person's data and be able to export it.

## Your vendors are in scope

Since 1 April 2025, a data processor that handles personal data for you (your CRM, email platform, web host, or ad agency) must follow the Security Principle itself. The responsibility stays with you, so ask them how they protect your data.

Most CRMs and email platforms store data outside Malaysia. Section 129 governs those transfers, and its whitelist is gone. If you are unsure whether your setup meets it, ask your DPO or a lawyer.

## You can't report on accounts you can't open

If a former vendor still holds your domain, hosting, or email accounts, you can't investigate a breach within 72 hours in accounts you can't log in to, and customer emails in staff members' free webmail are data you hold but don't control. The [[Website ownership checklist: who should hold your domain, hosting and analytics|website ownership checklist]] covers that side.

## Checklist

- Put the privacy notice on every form, in Malay and English.
- Make "stop contacting me" work in every channel, including WhatsApp and the sales team's own lists.
- Count the people in your CRM, email platform, and spreadsheets. Over 20,000 means a DPO.
- Write a one-page breach plan: who decides, who notifies the Commissioner, and where the register lives.
- Turn on two-factor authentication for the CRM, email platform, and ad accounts (see [[Business email compromise in Malaysia: how invoice fraud works]]).
- Stop exporting lists to personal accounts and phones.
- Remove former staff and agencies from every system on their last day.
- Ask each vendor how they secure your data, and keep the answer.

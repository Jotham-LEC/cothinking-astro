A small-business website in Malaysia costs about RM2,000 to RM3,300 as a five- or six-page package, and from about RM5,000 into the tens of thousands as a custom build from an agency, going by prices providers published in October 2026. Yearly costs for the domain, hosting, and maintenance come on top, and over five years they can exceed the build.

## Three price tiers

| Tier | Price | What you get |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Package | About RM2,000 to RM3,300 | A template, a fixed number of pages, your content dropped in; often a year or two of hosting |
| Custom, small agency | About RM5,000 to RM25,000 | A design made for you, more pages, and some integration (CRM, forms, a product catalogue) |
| Larger build | Above that; rarely published | Many pages, several languages, connections to an ERP or booking system, custom features |

For comparison, most Malaysian web developers listed on [Clutch](https://clutch.co/my/web-developers) show rates of US$25 to US$99 an hour.

## What moves the price

The same "corporate website" can cost RM3,000 or RM30,000. These are the usual reasons:

| What differs | How it moves the price |
| --- | --- |
| Pages and templates | Twenty pages on two layouts cost less than ten that all differ. |
| Template or custom design | A template is cheaper and quicker. A custom design costs more and fits better. |
| Copywriting | It is often excluded, and writing a good page takes longer than building it. |
| Integrations | A form that emails you is simple. One that creates a CRM lead and alerts sales on WhatsApp is a small project (see [[How to connect WhatsApp enquiries to your CRM]]). |
| A product catalogue or shop | Hundreds of products with specifications and filters take building, and the data has to be imported. |
| Languages | English, Bahasa Malaysia and Chinese mean three versions of every page. |
| Revisions | Two rounds per page is common, and every round after that is billed. |

## Costs a quote may leave out

Check each when you compare quotes; some hide in a renewal.

The domain renews every year. One web design firm lists a `.com.my` at RM85 a year and a `.my` at RM130, and the domain must be in your company's name (see [[Website ownership checklist: who should hold your domain, hosting and analytics]]).

Hosting is often free for the first year or two and then renews. One firm lists shared hosting at RM470 a year, and a hosting company's 5-page plan renews at RM462.

Premium themes and plugins usually renew yearly, often in US dollars. Elementor, a common WordPress page builder, publishes [paid plans](https://elementor.com/pricing/) "starting from $60/year", and four paid plugins mean four renewals.

Stock photos need licences, and extra rounds of revisions are billed by the hour.

Maintenance covers updates, backups, security and small changes, and the published examples differ widely: RM550 a year for 1 to 5 pages with 3 hours of changes, or RM1,500 a year for 6 to 10 pages with 10 hours (plus SST); a prepaid RM1,500 for 12 hours; and WordPress care plans from RM550 a month, with backups, updates and monitoring. [[What a website maintenance plan should include]] explains how to compare them.

## Compare the five-year cost

Compare five years, not the build:

```text
Five-year cost = build
               + 5 × (domain + hosting + licences)
               + 5 × maintenance
               + changes you will want (new pages, a campaign landing page)
```

| | Package | Custom build |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Build | RM2,000 | RM12,000 |
| Each year | RM500 of renewals, no maintenance | RM1,500 for hosting and maintenance |
| Five years | RM4,500 | RM19,500 |

Neither is wrong. They are different products, and the second should come with someone looking after it.

## Why the cheapest quote can cost more

A low quote is fine on its own; a package suits plenty of small companies. It becomes expensive when:

- the accounts are in the vendor's name, so leaving means a rebuild or a fight to get them back;
- it is built on a proprietary builder only that vendor can work on, so the next developer starts again;
- no one updates it, and out-of-date plugins are a common way into a small site, so a malware clean-up is paid for on the day, at whatever price the person available charges;
- the scope was vague, so every change after launch is billed as extra.

> [!TIP] Ask every provider for three numbers
> The build, the yearly cost after the free period, and what leaving would cost. Quotes most often leave out the third.

Ask for upkeep and new work to be priced separately: a known fee or known hours for routine care, and a price you approve before anything new. That keeps the yearly cost predictable.

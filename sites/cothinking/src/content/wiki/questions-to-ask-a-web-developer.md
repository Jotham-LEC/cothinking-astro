Send every developer on your shortlist the same twenty questions, in writing, and compare the answers side by side. Perfect answers are rare. What matters is that you understand each one and could hold the developer to it.

> [!TIP] Ask question 20 first
> A developer who can calmly describe how you would leave them is usually one you will want to stay with.

## Ownership and access

| Question | A good answer | A warning sign |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Whose name will the domain be registered in? | Your company's, on an account you own. | "Ours, it's easier." |
| 2. Whose account is the hosting on, and who pays for it? | Yours, with a user for them; or theirs, billed at cost and moved to you on request. | "It's on our server, it's included", until you leave. |
| 3. Who will be the administrator of the website, analytics, and Search Console? | Someone at your company, with the developer as a separate user. | "We'll send you a login": theirs, shared. |
| 4. Who owns the design, the code, and the content once the project is paid for? | You, in the contract, with licences in your name. | "We keep the source files; you get the website." |
| 5. Can I have a copy of the code and a backup at any time? | Yes, from a repository you can access or on request, free. | "Why would you need that?" |

The full list of accounts is in [[Website ownership checklist: who should hold your domain, hosting and analytics]].

## Scope and price

| Question | A good answer | A warning sign |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 6. What exactly is in the price? | A list of pages, features, integrations, and deliverables. | "A complete corporate website." |
| 7. What isn't in the price? | Named exclusions: copywriting, photography, licences, extra revisions. | "Everything's included." |
| 8. How many rounds of revisions do I get, and what counts as one? | A number, and a definition such as one list of changes per page. | "Unlimited", until it isn't. |
| 9. How do you charge for work outside the scope? | A rate, and a quote you approve first. | "We'll see when it comes up." |
| 10. What is the payment schedule? | Stages tied to milestones: deposit, design approved, launch. | 100% up front for a project of several months. |
| 11. Who writes the words and supplies the photos? | A clear answer, with a date for your part. | No answer: content is where projects stall. |

## Build and quality

| Question | A good answer | A warning sign |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 12. What will you build it with, and why? | A named platform and a reason that fits your brief and your editors. | "Our own system", which only they can work on. |
| 13. Can my team edit the pages? | What you can change, what needs them, and a short training session. | "Send us the changes", when you expected to edit. |
| 14. How will you make it fast and work on phones? | Image sizes, real phones, a speed check before launch. | "It's responsive." |
| 15. Will it be set up for search and for tracking enquiries? | Titles, descriptions, a sitemap, Search Console, analytics, and form tracking, in your accounts. | "SEO is a separate package." |
| 16. What happens to enquiries from the website's forms? | To an address you choose, tested; or to your CRM or WhatsApp. | No plan. |

A developer who asks how you name your campaign links is a good sign; see [[UTM parameters: a naming convention your team will keep]].

## After launch

| Question | A good answer | A warning sign |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 17. What support is included after launch, and for how long? | A period (30, 60, or 90 days); bugs yes, new features no. | "We'll always be here for you." |
| 18. What does maintenance cost afterwards, and what does it include? | A price, hours of changes, tested backups, updates, and reply times. | "RM X a year", with nothing about updates or backups. |
| 19. How fast do you reply, and how fast do you fix? | Two numbers, ideally by severity. A reply isn't a fix. | "ASAP." |
| 20. If we part ways, what do we get, and what does it cost? | Every login, a backup, and the code, within set days, at a stated fee or none. | A pause. |

[[What a website maintenance plan should include]] covers question 18 in detail.

## When the answers are close

Choose the developer whose answers you understood best and whose references are long-standing clients. If nobody answers questions 1 to 5 well, keep looking, or write the ownership terms into the contract yourself.

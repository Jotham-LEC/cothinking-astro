Most access problems start on someone's first or last day. These two checklists assume the setup in this series: one email suite, a password manager, shared drives, and an access register ([[Setting up IT for a small company, Part 2: The domain and accounts|Part 2]]). Skip the lines for systems you don't have.

## When someone joins

- [ ] Create their account in the email suite (`name@company.com.my`). Work is never done from a personal email address.
- [ ] Turn on two-factor authentication, and check that it works before they start.
- [ ] Invite them to the password manager, and give them only the shared vaults their role requires.
- [ ] Add them to the right groups (`sales@`, `accounts@`) and shared drives.
- [ ] Set up their laptop: encryption on, automatic updates on, password manager and browser installed ([[Setting up IT for a small company, Part 5: Laptops|Part 5]]).
- [ ] Give them their own login to the systems their department uses: the CRM for sales, the accounting system for finance, the stock or project system for operations.
- [ ] For banking, marketplaces, and government portals, create a login of their own where the service allows it, rather than sharing the owner's.
- [ ] Record each account in the access register.

## When someone leaves

- [ ] On their last day, suspend their email account and reset its password. Don't delete it yet.
- [ ] Remove them from the password manager, then change every shared password they could see.
- [ ] Transfer their files and mailbox to a manager, then delete the account. In Google Workspace, transfer the data before deleting the user; in Microsoft 365, convert the mailbox to a shared mailbox.
- [ ] Remove them from the department systems, and reassign their records: customers and open deals in the CRM, open invoices in the accounting system, open tasks elsewhere.
- [ ] Remove their bank approval device, marketplace sub-accounts, and portal logins.
- [ ] Collect the laptop, wipe it, and reinstall it.
- [ ] Forward their email address, or set an automatic reply, for a few months.
- [ ] Work through the access register and confirm that every line has been closed.

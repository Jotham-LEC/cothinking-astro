Every job I do has a fixed price, which I put in writing before I start. You pay it once the job is done, on one bill at the end of the month, with no prepaid hours and no contract term.

## Owners ask what a job costs

For a while I billed by the hour, as accountants and lawyers do. It seemed the fairest arrangement, since you pay for the time the work takes and nothing more. The owners I worked with saw it differently. Few of them asked what I charged an hour; they asked what the job would cost, because that is the number they have to approve and plan around. An hourly rate answers a question they weren't asking and leaves open the one they were.

## Prepaid plans charge for hours you don't use

The other common arrangement is a fixed monthly fee for a set number of hours, often with a six- or twelve-month minimum. A small company's problems come in bursts: one month the emails stop reaching customers, and for the next three nothing breaks. The plan charges the same in all four months, and unused hours rarely carry over. If you are offered one, [[What a website maintenance plan should include]] says what to check.

## How it works

1. You send me the problem.
2. I reply with what I think is wrong and a fixed price for fixing it.
3. Once you approve the price, I do the work.
4. At the end of the month you get one bill for the jobs I finished, at the prices you approved.

If the work takes me longer than I expected, the price doesn't change. It changes only if the job does, for example if you ask for more, or if something turns out to be different from what we both understood, such as ten mailboxes rather than five. In that case I stop and send a new price before going further.

I still record the time every job takes. That record is what keeps my prices honest, and it tells me when a kind of job is priced too high or too low.

A quiet month costs nothing. My only monthly fee is an optional Care Plan for the upkeep of a site I built, and changes under it are priced like any other job. Prices and terms are on [How I work](/how-i-work).

## It isn't the cheapest for everyone

A fixed price carries a margin for the things that go wrong, so a job that runs smoothly would have cost a little less by the hour. That is the cost of knowing the price in advance, and most owners I work with would rather pay it. If you need many hours of work every month, an in-house IT person or a provider with a dedicated team may cost less.

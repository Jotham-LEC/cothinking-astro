When people search for what you sell nearby, Google shows a map of businesses before most websites. To appear there, and higher in the results below it, a business needs a verified Google Business Profile with its details filled in, and a website Google can read.

## How to check

Signs you have this problem:

- Searching your company's name doesn't bring up your business on the map.
- Your listing has old hours, a wrong number, or photos you didn't choose.
- A former employee or an old agency controls it.

To confirm it:

1. On your phone, search your company's name and town on Google Maps.
2. Search `site:yourdomain.com.my` on Google to see how many of your website's pages it knows.
3. Sign in to [Google Business Profile](https://business.google.com/) with the company's Google account to see which profiles you manage.

> [!TIP] Free tools
> - [Google Business Profile](https://business.google.com/): the listing itself, free.
> - [Google Search Console](https://search.google.com/search-console): shows which of your pages Google has listed, and why it skipped the rest.
> - [PageSpeed Insights](https://pagespeed.web.dev/): how fast your site loads on a phone, which affects how Google ranks it.

## Possible causes

- There is no profile, or nobody can reach the one there is. Many were set up years ago by someone who has since left.
- There are duplicate listings. Two profiles for one business split its reviews and confuse Google.
- Details are missing. A profile without a category, opening hours, photos or a website is shown less often.
- Google can't read the website. A site that blocks Google or has no sitemap may not be listed at all.

## If you or your IT person want to do it yourselves

### Before you start

You need:

- **A Google account the company owns**, not a personal one, so the profile stays when people leave.
- **Proof of the business** for verification: your SSM registration, signage, the shopfront or workplace, tools of the trade.
- **The details customers use**: the address (or the area you serve), phone number, opening hours and website.
- **A login to your DNS or website**, to verify Search Console.

### How to fix it

#### 1. Find or claim the profile

1. Search for your business on Google Maps.
2. If it is there, choose **Own this business?** or **Claim this business**.
3. If someone else owns it, request access. Google emails the current owner; if they don't reply within a few days, Google may let you claim it.

#### 2. Create a profile if there is none

At [business.google.com](https://business.google.com/), choose **Add business**, then your business's name, its main category, and either its address or the area you serve.

#### 3. Verify it

Google picks the method: a phone call or text, an email, a video, or a postcard. For a video, record one take showing your signage, the street outside, and something only the owner can open, such as the till or the office.

#### 4. Fill it in

- **Primary category**: the one that matters most. Pick the closest match to what you sell.
- **Hours, phone, website, services and photos**: all of them.
- **The name**: your business's real name, nothing more. Adding keywords breaks Google's rules and can get the profile suspended.

#### 5. Make your website readable to Google

1. Add your domain to Search Console and verify it with the DNS record it gives you.
2. Submit your sitemap, usually `yourdomain.com.my/sitemap.xml`.
3. Open the **Pages** report and fix what it says isn't indexed.

#### 6. Ask for reviews

Share the review link from your profile with happy customers, and reply to every review, good or bad.

## Questions

### Is it free?

Yes. The profile and Search Console cost nothing.

### Can anyone get me to the top of Google?

Nobody can honestly promise that. What most small businesses lack is a complete, verified profile and a site Google can read, and both are in your hands.

> I gave commentary on China's new guidelines for making physical stores places to spend time, not just to shop, and what they mean for retailers' margins, financing and private label. [Read the full article on Retail Asia](https://retailasia.com/exclusive/china-retailers-spruce-stores-price-wars-persist) (Jaleen Ramos, September 14, 2026).

Jotham Lim, head of global marketing at Beijing Taomi Technology Co. Ltd., said the plan does not ask retailers to stop competing on price. “They are asking stores to be worth visiting for reasons beyond price,” he said in an emailed reply to questions.

“It shifts the industry from betting on total foot traffic to increasing margins,” he said. “Experience raises the fixed cost per square metre, so it pays where footfall converts and hurts where it doesn't.”

“The guidelines are not asking offline retailers to beat online retail,” he said. “They are asking offline retail to become digitally capable.”

“Private label is the part I would pay special attention to, because it lets retailers compete on value without cutting the headline price,” Lim said.

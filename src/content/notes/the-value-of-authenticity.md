I have invested heavily in making brands appear genuine and authentic, both for my personal brands and the corporate ones I manage.

Coming from a journalistic background, I held onto the belief that being authentic, being genuine, and having a good story is what makes a brand great. Many marketing books and philosophies adopt this belief, after all. 

But years have gone by, and while those strategies may have worked when times were great; Under economic distress, branding is but a thin wrapper around a product and is no longer a value proposition in itself.

People and companies today are more discerning, more skeptical.

Everything is a product—myself included. Market axioms remain consistent: to extract the most value with the least amount of resources. If my skills do not fill a talent gap, or if my company's services do not resolve a pain point, we, as products, are irrelevant.

Thankfully, the situation has changed, but the strategy has largely remained the same.

To survive and grow is simply a series of identifying and addressing bottlenecks. Sometimes, our ability to deliver value is lacking; other times, it is our visibility in the market.

So far, I believe that I have balanced them quite well. For the past few years, I have honed builder skills that keep me relevant in a post-AI landscape, and I am trying to make my efforts visible and clear.

It is hard work, but it is foundational. I can only hope for a little luck to come my way.

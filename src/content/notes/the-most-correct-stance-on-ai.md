> Is AI the harbinger of death for all white collar work? Or is it still glorified autocomplete: no better than a talented but misguided, overconfident intern?

I've consumed a lot of content from both sides of the aisle, and I'm done flip-flopping between optimism and existential dread. It's about time I came to my own conclusion and moved on.

Yes, the technology is amazing. It has revolutionised almost every part of the economy and daily life, and brought technical, ethical, environmental and legal problems with it. But we've dealt with problems like these before, and we'll deal with them just as we did with motor vehicles, the nuclear bomb and the early days of the internet.

No, growth will not accelerate forever. The bubble will pop, and the impact will only decline from here on out, unless there's a mega breakthrough beyond agentic coding.

Yes, your job is at risk. When writing code costs pennies, the value moves up the chain to system architecture, design and actually solving the problem. If your day job is turning requirements into code by hand, AI will do it cheaper, faster and often better.

No, that doesn't mean there's no value in learning to write code or produce content by hand. Expertise is still valued, and your white-collar job won't be fully replaced, but the work will certainly change. There will be more… entropy. Product and project managers will do more design and development, and developers and designers will take on more product and project work.

Yes, the better you are at your profession, the more useless AI is to you. I'm always frustrated with how AI writes, and I'd rather write my essays myself. In the same way, seasoned developers don't see how AI is more useful than a linter, a language server, a formatter or snippets. If anything, it makes the codebase harder to understand.

No, you're not helpless without AI. Even if your job is made entirely redundant, there will always be other work to do. I recently ran out of tokens, went through my to-do list, and realised I had a lot of non-AI work that needed doing. If you're helpless without AI or an internet connection, it's time to re-evaluate your position.

Yes, open-source maintainers are allowed to auto-ban AI-generated pull requests. It's their right, and it's how they hold back a flood of low-quality submissions.

No, you can't vibe your way to a high-quality product. Quality comes from care, intention and a long series of small decisions. After all, that's what makes a luxury car, watch or bag what it is.

## TL:DR

The neutral take is the boring take. But I think it's also the most correct one, because the subject is nuanced, complicated and still changing.

AI isn't a fad, and it isn't the best thing since sliced bread either. It's only as useful as the person using it. It levels the playing field, but reality hasn't changed much. Demand for quality craftsmanship has only gone up, the bar keeps rising, and the talented and dedicated will survive.

Chaos and change are the default; a long stasis is the statistical anomaly. Developers can't expect their black-box codebases to keep them in highly paid, cushy jobs forever.

The survival strategy has always been the same: find the bottleneck, get past it, rinse and repeat. Turning ideas into code used to be a bottleneck. Now the bottleneck is somewhere else.
